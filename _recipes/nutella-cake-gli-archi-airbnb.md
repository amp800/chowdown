directions:
- 'Start preparing the Monte Amiata Ricciolina Cake with the shortcrust pastry: in the mixer, with the whisk k mounted, mix
  the sugar with the butter in cubes. Then add the egg, the egg yolks and a pinch of salt, followed by the flour and the yeast.
  Knead everything quickly until you obtain a smooth dough.'
- 'Give it the shape of a loaf, wrap in cling film and place in the refrigerator for at least 30 minutes. In the meantime,
  dedicate yourself to the filling: in a saucepan, mix the egg, sugar and flour with a whisk, then gradually add the milk
  brought to the point of boiling. Make sure that no lumps form.'
- Place on low heat and cook, stirring constantly, until the cream thickens. Transfer the cream to a bowl and let it cool.
  Then add the chocolate cream and the roughly chopped dried fruit and mix until the mixture is well blended.
- 'Take the shortcrust pastry and roll out half of it with a rolling pin on a lightly floured surface: you need to bring it
  to a thickness of about 5 mm. Place it inside a 22 cm diameter springform pan, buttered and floured. Trim the edges so that
  they are about 4 cm high and prick the base. Spread the chocolate and dried fruit cream on top and level it. Roll out the
  remaining shortcrust pastry to the same thickness and cover the filling, sealing the edges well. Trim the circumference
  of the cake again.'
- Bake the cake in a preheated oven at 180° for 40 minutes or until the surface is golden brown. Remove the cake from the
  oven and let it cool completely before gently removing it from the mold. When the cake is cold, beat the egg whites with
  the sugar using an electric mixer until the mixture is shiny and fluffy. Spread it over the surface of the cake and decorate
  with melted chocolate dripped in thin threads. Place the Monte Amiata Ricciolina cake back in the oven at 200° for 1 minute.
  Remove from the oven, let it cool and serve.
